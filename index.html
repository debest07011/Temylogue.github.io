The Room Without Sound

There’s a room I return to often. It has no doors, no clocks, no music.

  • Full Story

There’s a room I return to often. It has no doors, no clocks, no music. Just me — and the echo of every thought I’ve ever left unfinished. In this room, silence is not absence — it’s presence. It breathes. It watches. It holds a mirror up to everything I pretend not to feel.

At first, I ran from the quiet. I filled it with screens, with noise, with motion. But I’ve learned something — the things we run from usually carry the answers we’re chasing. The silence isn’t empty. It’s full. Full of memories, regrets, dreams too shy to speak out loud.

When you sit in a room without sound, you begin to hear things that were always there: the weight of unsaid words. The pulse of your own aliveness. The whispered voice of truth — asking only to be noticed.

This is not a post. It’s a pause. Welcome to the room.

To Be Soft in a Hard World

Being soft is not weakness. It’s a quiet kind of power.

  • Full Story

Being soft is not weakness. It’s a quiet kind of power.

To stay kind when the world sharpens its edges… to remain tender when everything teaches you to harden… that is the kind of strength that doesn’t shout, but shapes.

We are told to be unbothered. To be cool, composed, untouchable. But real courage looks different. It cries in public. It says "I love you" first. It apologizes when it’s easier not to. It lets itself be seen.

This piece is for the gentle ones. The empaths. The feelers.
The ones who flinch at cruelty but still choose to show up with open hands.

To be soft in a hard world is revolutionary.
And you, my friend, are a quiet revolution in motion.

The Unwritten Things

Some stories don’t want to be told.

  • Full Story

Some stories don’t want to be told.

They linger just outside the reach of language — on the edge of dreams and déjà vu. Not because they’re not ready, but because maybe they’re meant to remain open. Soft. Incomplete.

I’ve written entire paragraphs only to delete them, not because they weren’t beautiful, but because they weren’t true enough.

There are moments that feel like stories: a glance held a second too long, a word swallowed instead of spoken, the way the sky shifts when you’re thinking of someone. These are the unwritten things — and they matter.

This space isn’t for conclusions.
It’s for wondering. Wandering.
A place where the reader finishes the sentence with their own heart.

Somewhere in these fragments, I hope you find yourself.

A Letter to the Self I Lost

Hey. I miss you.

  • Full Story

Hey.
I miss you.

I know we don’t talk much anymore.
Life got louder. We got busy. And somewhere between who I was and who I was supposed to be… I let go of you.

You used to laugh without filtering the sound. You trusted people before overthinking it. You danced without wondering how you looked. You believed in magic — not as a metaphor, but as something real and possible.

I lost you in the pursuit of becoming “better.”
Smarter. Tougher. More productive.
But I didn’t realize that in letting go of your softness, I was losing the most sacred part of myself.

So this letter is a small attempt to come back.
Not to who I was, but to the parts of me that still whisper in old songs, in childhood dreams, in the way I sometimes still look at the stars.

If you’re still in there — I’m listening.

Love,
The me who never really stopped needing you.

The Grief No One Talks About

Nobody warned me

Nobody warned me that becoming would feel like a kind of death.

When we talk about grief, we talk about loss — of people, moments, certainty. But what about the version of you that you dreamed of in quiet confidence? The one you thought you’d grow into? The one you thought you’d have become by now?

There is a silent mourning in realizing your life looks nothing like the blueprint. That you’ve taken roads you never meant to walk, and left behind pieces of yourself that never got to fully bloom.

I used to think grief was a funeral.
But it can also be a Tuesday, staring at your reflection, thinking: “This isn’t what I pictured.”

And yet, even in that grief, there is grace. Because you’re still here. Still unfolding. Still becoming — just not in the way you expected.

And maybe that’s the point.

Rest Without Guilt

You are not a machine.

You weren’t made to run forever.

But somewhere along the line, the world convinced you that exhaustion equals excellence. That if you aren’t busy, you aren’t worthy. So now, you only stop when your body breaks — and even then, you feel ashamed.

You've been taught to apologize for rest. For stillness. For silence. You feel guilty for choosing peace over performance, breath over burnout. But rest is not laziness. It's not weakness. It's not something you must earn.

Even the sun disappears. Even oceans retreat. Even God — who needs nothing — chose to rest.

So why can’t you?

Rest is a return. To yourself. To your softness. To the quiet voice you keep drowning out with deadlines.

It’s where healing begins. Where your spirit exhales. Where your heart remembers that it matters too.

So close the laptop. Turn off the noise. Say no — not because you’re weak, but because you’ve finally remembered: you are not a machine.

You are allowed to pause. Without guilt. Without apology.

Rest without guilt. Because your value isn’t measured by your productivity. It’s written in your being — and being, sometimes, is enough.

The Applause Is a Distraction

Applause is sweet — deceptively so.

  • Full Story

It comes fast and loud, echoing off the walls of your effort, your exhaustion, your desperate reaching. It makes you feel seen, validated, and maybe — if only for a moment — like you matter.

But applause is a shallow river. It touches the surface, never the soul.

They clap for the version of you that performs well.
They clap for the version of you that smiles, delivers, survives.
But the applause does not sit beside you on your sleepless nights.
It does not ask how your soul is weathering the storm of always being “on.”

And yet, we chase it.
We build lives around it.
We shape our words, our dreams, our very selves to fit the mold that earns the loudest cheers.

But here’s the haunting truth:
You can gain all the applause in the world and still feel completely unseen.

Because they aren’t clapping for your healing.
They don’t cheer for your boundaries.
They don’t celebrate when you cancel plans to protect your peace.

And so you keep running. Keep pushing. Keep achieving.
But inside, you’re starving — not for sound, but for silence.
Not for being noticed, but for being understood.
Not for noise, but for peace.

Sometimes, silence is the loudest kind of affirmation.
Sometimes, the absence of applause is where you finally meet yourself — raw, unfinished, and finally honest.

The applause is a distraction if it leads you away from who you really are.

So take the stage if you must — but know when to step off.
And never forget: your soul is not a performance.

The Place of Worship

Sacred Presence, Silent Absence

We enter mosques. We enter churches. We sit, we kneel, we recite. The rituals are beautiful — but sometimes, our hearts are somewhere else entirely.

Because you can sing the hymns. You can recite the verses. You can even raise your hands in prayer — and still feel hollow inside.

We often confuse presence with surrender. But God is not measuring your footsteps into the building. He’s watching what you brought with you — your grief, your guilt, your gratitude, your honesty.

Real worship isn't in the posture of the body, but in the bending of the ego, the breaking of pride, the whisper that says, “I need You, still.”

So the question remains — Have I entered the house of worship, but locked the door to my own heart?

All Men Have Tongues

And They’re Sharpening Them

There is no such thing as neutral speech.

Everyone is sharpening their tongue — whether they know it or not. Some sharpen it with books and wisdom. Others sharpen it with hate, with envy, with shallow opinions turned loud.

In a world of many voices, we must ask: What is your tongue trained to do? Heal? Or humiliate?

Because every conversation is a choice. To uplift or to unravel. To pour light or pour fuel on someone else’s fire.

We’re all being listened to. Even in silence, even in sarcasm, even in quick online comments. Someone hears. Someone carries it. Sometimes forever.

And while your words may feel temporary, the shame, the damage, the insecurity they plant — may take years to undo.

So speak gently. Because everyone has a tongue. But not everyone has learned when to keep it still.
